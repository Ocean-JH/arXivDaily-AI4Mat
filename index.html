    Necessary conditions for benchmarking next-generation AI in materials discoveryNew

    Authors
    Daniel E Widdowson, Tatiana V Kurlina, Olga D Anosova, Vitaliy A Kurlin
    Published
    2026-10-09
    Category
    cond-mat.mtrl-sci
    arXiv ID
    2610.13885v1
    Open on arXiv ↗
    Read abstract

    Current AI predicts materials by speeding up energy minimisation and training on large databases. However, scaling data generation prevents manual analysis by domain experts. If novelty is evaluated by chemical compositions or traditional representations in Crystallographic Information Files, or standard structure matchers based on manually chosen thresholds, one can easily generate millions of ``new'' compositions or files without discovering truly novel materials. This paper uses a hierarchy of crystal invariants to continuously trace and quantify structural novelty based on the new mathematical definition of a crystal structure and asymptotic guarantees for invariant descriptors. The invariant-based analysis through cascade computations on a modest computer exposes the unexpected scale of duplication in the world's largest materials databases, shown here in the high-profile case of GNoME datasets. To prevent current ``embarrassment of over-prediction'', we propose necessary conditions for next-generation AI that should be automatically enforced before claiming any new materials.

    Doubly Metastable Synthesis for Accelerated Quantum Materials DiscoveryNew

    Authors
    Kyuho Lee, Berit H. Goodge, Soonhyun Kwon, Yash Anand, Theo Manev, Maximilien F. Debbas, Weiliang Luo, Takehito Suzuki, Heather J. Kulik, Joseph G. Checkelsky
    Published
    2026-10-09
    Category
    cond-mat.mtrl-sci
    arXiv ID
    2610.13859v1
    Open on arXiv ↗
    Read abstract

    New physics emerges from new materials, which in turn rely on the development of new synthesis techniques. Identifying the enlargement of metastable synthesis window as enabling for realizing novel materials, we develop diffusion-activated epitaxy, a technique which combines two metastable synthesis methods of diffusion and epitaxy. We observe that within a narrow window of investigated synthesis space we can experimentally identify the stabilization of four distinct phases, three of which show X-ray diffraction and scanning transmission electron microscopy characterization data that are incompatible with the crystal structures registered in experimental and computational inorganic structure databases. These results suggest an enlarged metastable synthesis window is enabled by this synthesis approach, identifying doubly metastable synthesis as a promising method for exploration of quantum materials.

    Predictive Synthesis of MOFs and COFsNew

    Authors
    Bin Feng, Reid A. Coyle, Shyam Chand Pal, Peter Walther, Saeun Park, Zhiling Zheng
    Published
    2026-10-08
    Category
    physics.chem-ph
    arXiv ID
    2610.12739v1
    Open on arXiv ↗
    Read abstract

    Predictive synthesis concerns the prospective modeling of how a targeted structure can be realized. The synthesis of metal-organic frameworks (MOFs) and covalent organic frameworks (COFs) intrinsically involves a multidimensional space of interdependent variables, posing a challenge for bench chemists to parameterize and predict. In this Perspective, we discuss the evolution from chemical-intuition-driven experimentation toward the integration of artificial intelligence (AI) and machine learning (ML) in the laboratory. By virtue of prior knowledge, uncertainty, and experimental failure in determining what should be attempted next, we envision predictive synthesis progressing from static recipe recommendation toward real-time control and transferable principles of framework formation. In this context, the significance lies in transforming trial-and-error practice into a means of measurable chemical reasoning, thereby accelerating crystalline materials discovery and advancing synthetic chemistry.
