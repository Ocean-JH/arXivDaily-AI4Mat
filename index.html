    Learning Nonlocal Interactions in Large-Scale Heterogeneous Metamaterials through Scientific Machine LearningNew

    Authors
    Dex Doksoo Lee, Jehyeon Shin, Yue Yu, Sunkyu Yu, Junsuk Rho, Wei Chen
    Published
    2026-10-30
    Category
    cs.CE
    arXiv ID
    2610.41887v1
    Open on arXiv ↗
    Read abstract

    In multiscale systems, nonlocal interactions among spatially distributed constituents can unlock collective, potentially emergent behavior irreducible to individual constituent responses. Beyond characterization, nonlocality in multiscale systems is increasingly viewed as a design element across fracture mechanics, wave physics, and disordered systems. Existing nonlocality theories provide rigorous descriptions and mechanistic insight, but often require prescribed analytic forms, range, symmetries, or periodicity. Machine learning offers a data-driven route to learn interactions, yet principled approaches that tie the network design to physical theory remain nascent. Here we present domain-decomposition attention neural operator (DANO), which learns configuration-dependent interactions in heterogeneous systems governed by linear field equations from field data alone. Through a case study in large-scale optical metasurfaces, we demonstrate DANO's full-field prediction under geometric heterogeneity and across drive conditions, small-to-large domain transfer, and multifunctional inverse design, with learned interactions validated through physics-based interrogation. As an AI-for-Science tool, it supports interaction discovery and scalable, customizable design of nonlocal multiscale systems, including virtual-reality optics.
