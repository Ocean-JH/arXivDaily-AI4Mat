    Precursor Ratio-Controlled Chloride Vacancies in Lead-Free KCuCl3-x Memristors for Forming-Free Gradual Resistive Switching and Neuromorphic LearningNew

    Authors
    Chinmayee Mandar Mhaskar, Sangeeta Roy Chaudhuri, Ayan Roy Chaudhuri
    Published
    2026-10-26
    Category
    cond-mat.mtrl-sci
    arXiv ID
    2610.35436v1
    Open on arXiv ↗
    Read abstract

    Defect engineering of memristive materials offers a powerful strategy for regulating ionic transport and analog resistive switching required for neuromorphic computing. Here, we report a wet chemical synthesis route for chloride-vacancy (VCl) engineering in lead-free all-inorganic KCuCl3-x thin films and establish a direct correlation between defect concentration, electronic structure, transport dynamics, and neuromorphic performance. Controlled precursor-ratio variation systematically tunes the VCl concentration in KCuCl3-x thin films, driving preferential vacancy formation at the terminal chloride sites, band-gap narrowing, and enhanced electronic disorder. W/KCuCl3-x/FTO memristors exhibit stable bipolar VCM-type resistive switching, with increasing VCl concentration promoting forming-free operation. An optimal VCl concentration (~ 1.3*10^21 cm^-3) provides a balance between ionic mobility and defect controllability, resulting in highly linear potentiation/depression characteristics and superior pulse endurance (>104 cycles). When the experimentally measured conductance characteristics of the optimized memristor are implemented as synaptic weights in artificial neural network simulations, recognition accuracies of 98.96% and 91.82% are achieved on the MNIST and Fashion-MNIST datasets, respectively. These findings establish precursor ratio-controlled VCl engineering as an effective strategy for simultaneously optimizing ionic transport, analog switching, and neuromorphic learning in lead-free halide perovskite memristors, providing a general materials design principle for energy-efficient neuromorphic electronics.

    SymADiT: Symmetry-Aware Crystal Generation with Latent Flow MatchingNew

    Authors
    Anmar Karmush, Cedric Mathieu Brandenburg, Soheil Ershadrad, Johanna Rosen, Michael Felsberg, Filip Ekström Kelvinius
    Published
    2026-05-11
    Category
    cs.LG
    arXiv ID
    2605.10115v2
    Open on arXiv ↗
    Read abstract

    Tackling the task of crystal generation, we aim to enhance the previously proposed, symmetry-agnostic, All-atom Diffusion Transformer (ADiT) by introducing SymADiT, a symmetry-aware variant. To do so, we use a representation of crystals based on Wyckoff positions. We follow ADiT and perform generative modelling in latent space, adapted to our symmetry-aware representation. By forcing the output of the generative model to adhere to the symmetry restrictions imposed by the generated crystal's space group and each atom's Wyckoff position, the generated crystals exhibit more realistic symmetry properties. We benchmark our method against both symmetry-aware and symmetry-agnostic models for crystal generation and show competitive performance, generating stable, symmetric, and physically plausible crystals with only an off-the-shelf Transformer architecture. Our code is available at https://github.com/Karmush21/SymADiT.

    From Autonomous Knowledge Generation to Generalizable Property Prediction: A Precision-First Pipeline for Electro-Optic Materials DiscoveryNew

    Authors
    Dibakar Datta
    Published
    2026-10-25
    Category
    cond-mat.mtrl-sci
    arXiv ID
    2610.34628v1
    Open on arXiv ↗
    Read abstract

    Electro-optic materials, whose refractive index changes linearly with an applied electric field (the Pockels effect), set the speed and energy of optical modulators and microwave-to-optical links for quantum computing. Designing better materials requires optimizing composition alongside temperature, strain, and bias field. However, general repositories lack electro-optic tensor data. We present a pipeline that builds such a database from literature following the rule: "no value is better than a wrong value." The pipeline has five stages: physics-aware acquisition, layout-aware extraction via LLM and deterministic table parsers, a three-tier verification gate, cross-paper consensus using median and median absolute deviation, and a noise-robust surrogate model audited by leave-one-material-out (LOMO) testing. Physics enters directly: notation is mapped, point-group symmetry-forbidden Pockels components are rejected, equivalent labels are merged, and values carry provenance tags. In a run over 49 publications, the gate processed 816 candidates into 414 accepted, 215 held for review, and 187 rejected. Against a hand-certified gold standard of 303 values from 16 papers, the run achieved a precision of 0.51 and recall of 0.48 for exact record matches. Across four database snapshots, held-out materials where the surrogate model beat a predict-the-mean baseline grew from 1 of 4 to 9 of 13 as the database expanded from 10 to 22 materials. While not a controlled comparison, this trend supports the premise that a precision-oriented, physics-checked database can power forward models beyond its training set and clarifies requirements for inverse material design.
