    Diagnostic Accuracy Is Not Deployment Value: Decision-Aligned Reliability Evaluation for Foundation MLIPs in Materials Discovery

    Authors
    Harry Sevi, Joonatan E. M. Laulainen
    Published
    2026-11-23
    Category
    cs.LG
    arXiv ID
    2611.30066v1
    Open on arXiv ↗
    Read abstract

    In AI-guided materials discovery, machine-learning models increasingly decide which candidate materials should receive expensive first-principles or experimental evaluation. Standard reliability metrics such as uncertainty, calibration, novelty, and ranking accuracy evaluate properties of the predictions, but success on these metrics does not necessarily imply a better downstream screening decision. We study this mismatch for foundation machine-learned interatomic potentials (MACE-MP, CHGNet, Orb V3) used to rank candidate crystal structures before DFT verification. We evaluate three representative reliability interventions (novelty detection, sparse DFT calibration, and chemistry-level ranking audits) using a deployment objective that measures how much DFT can be avoided while maintaining a required recall of near-hull candidates. Across all three, a reliability signal can succeed on its immediate target without improving the final screening decision: novelty detects distribution shift but weakly ranks failure severity; calibration can improve pooled ranking metrics while leaving screening unchanged or degrading it; and sparse audits identify MLIP-chemistry combinations with poor ranking reliability without yielding a robustly beneficial routing policy. A two-oracle analysis further shows that the source of failure depends on the screening decision itself: under formation-energy screening, even perfect knowledge of average ranking reliability can select the wrong model, whereas in the evaluated hull-aligned variant the oracle target becomes decision-aligned but finite-budget audits still fail to realize the value. Reliability should be evaluated relative to the decision it supports: the relevant question is not whether a model is uncertain or inaccurate, but whether acquiring and acting on additional information will improve the final discovery decision enough to justify its cost.

    Unveiling the Complex high-pressure phases of Sodium at the Melting Curve Minimum via Data-Drive Crystal Structure Prediction

    Authors
    Zhenyu Wang, Chengqian Zhang, Xiaoyang Wang, Haiyang Niu, Jian Lv, Han Wang
    Published
    2026-11-23
    Category
    cond-mat.mtrl-sci
    arXiv ID
    2611.29889v1
    Open on arXiv ↗
    Read abstract

    Sodium exhibits a remarkable concentration of complex crystalline phases near its melting-curve minimum, yet the atomic arrangements of several experimentally observed phases remain unresolved. Here, we combine CALYPSO crystal structure prediction with Deep Potential models developed through concurrent learning to explore this structural landscape. Searches over more than 1.6 million configurations recover the previously reported $I4/m$ structure of $tI50$ and identify candidate atomic arrangements for $oC120$ and $aP90$, together with three additional low-enthalpy structures, $tI52$, $oP72$, and $oP92$. The proposed candidates reproduce key features of the experimentally indexed cell geometries. First-principles enthalpy calculations, Gibbs free-energy comparisons, and solid--liquid coexistence simulations reveal close competition among these structures and established sodium phases. Near the calculated reference melting minimum, $tI50$ is favored over $cI16$ and $oP8$, whereas $oC120$ and $aP90$ remain closely competitive, with their relative stability sensitive to model accuracy. These results provide testable structural models for unresolved experimental phases and reveal multiple closely competing low-enthalpy configurations near sodium's melting minimum

    Generating grain boundary structures at a specified energy with a symmetry-constrained diffusion model

    Authors
    Kazuya Miyamoto, Ibuki Okuda, Izumi Takahara, Teruyasu Mizoguchi
    Published
    2026-11-20
    Category
    cond-mat.mtrl-sci
    arXiv ID
    2611.27188v1
    Open on arXiv ↗
    Read abstract

    Grain boundaries (GBs) govern many properties of polycrystalline materials, and inverse design would make the GB structure itself a design variable. Crystal generative models such as MatterGen operate on periodic cells, which contain two GBs. The cell energy defines a single GB only when the two are equivalent, but existing models do not enforce this equivalence. Building on MatterGen, we developed a symmetry-constrained diffusion model in which the two GBs are related by a symmetry operation: atoms near one GB are generated, and those near the other are reconstructed through a symmetry projection applied at every denoising step. We applied it to the $Σ5(210)[001]$ symmetric tilt GB in Al, conditioning generation on the GB energy from a machine-learning interatomic potential (MLIP). All 350 generated structures kept a geometrically equivalent GB pair after relaxation. Given a target GB energy, the model generated structures at that energy: for the three in-range targets with many nearby training structures, 44% to 70% fell within $\pm 0.1$ J/m$^2$ of the target. Tracking weakened at higher energies and failed outside the training range. Of the 91 near-target structures, 90 matched no training structure under the adopted geometric criterion, indicating that the model can propose configurations near the target energy that are distinct from the training structures, a capability that could help identify unknown metastable GB structures observed in experiments. Building GB-pair equivalence into generation thus makes GB properties well-defined design targets, enabling inverse design of GB structures.

    Physics-Guided Crystal Structure Elucidation from Powder X-Ray Diffraction with Universal Machine-Learning Interatomic Potentials

    Authors
    Yunsung Lim, Seungwoo Hwang, Seungwu Han
    Published
    2026-11-18
    Category
    cond-mat.mtrl-sci
    arXiv ID
    2611.23760v1
    Open on arXiv ↗
    Read abstract

    Determining unique atomic coordinates from powder X-ray diffraction (PXRD) remains challenging because peak overlap, weak scattering from light elements, limited scattering contrast between elements with similar atomic numbers, and experimental noise render the inverse problem underdetermined. Here, we present XCAPE (XRD-Constrained Accelerated Prediction Engine), a crystal structure prediction (CSP) framework that integrates experimentally derived composition, unit cell parameters, space group, and PXRD data into a symmetry-aware evolutionary search. XCAPE explores Wyckoff-site configurations using tailored mutation operations, evaluates candidates by both energetic stability and diffraction agreement, and accelerates geometry optimization using a universal machine-learning interatomic potential (MLIP). The framework recovered 71 of 77 database-derived benchmark structures (92.2%) from simulated PXRD patterns and all 12 reference structures from experimental PXRD patterns. It further identified 12 candidate structure assignments that are consistent with experimental PXRD patterns, lack matching entries in the Inorganic Crystal Structure Database (ICSD), and are thermodynamically competitive at the MLIP level. Density functional theory (DFT) calculations showed that 10 of these 12 candidates lie within 10 meV/atom of the convex hull. By bridging experimental diffraction data and physically plausible atomistic models, XCAPE can provide structural insight into previously unresolved materials and support accelerated materials discovery in automated research workflows.

    Authors
    Derek van Tilborg, Laura-Bianca Pasca, Harry Sevi, Kyle Reeves, Thomas Loux, Aikaterini Flessa Savvidou, Varincka Jean-Gilles, Marilou Carouge, Joonatan E. M. Laulainen
    Published
    2026-11-17
    Category
    cs.CE
    arXiv ID
    2611.22623v1
    Open on arXiv ↗
    Read abstract

    Inorganic materials discovery is increasingly limited by synthesis rather than by material design. Large language models (LLMs) are being adopted to propose synthesis conditions with little experimental evidence of their capabilities and biases. Here, we carry out an experimental synthesis benchmark of 240 experiments across eight known and unknown inorganic oxides and subsequently evaluate an LLM as a zero-shot ranker over fixed banks of solid-state synthesis recipes. For each pair of recipes for the same oxide target, `GPT-5.6-Sol` selects the recipe expected to yield higher target-phase purity. We aggregate these pairwise preferences using a regularized Bradley-Terry model to obtain a ranked list of synthesis recipes for a given target material. By comparison with the actual purity, obtained through X-ray diffraction, we measure `GPT-5.6-Sol`'s performance as a zero-shot ranker (0.72 pairwise accuracy). By selective removal of the information exposed to the model during this ranking, we conclude that ranking is driven predominantly by thermal features, with reaction chemistry modulating the effect. Ranking accuracy tracks the purity-temperature correlation by target (Spearman ρ= 0.91\), \(p = 0.002\), and on the six thermally-driven targets withholding all chemical identity hardly impacts performance (0.68) compared to withholding temperature (0.56). Ranking performance was higher on known than unknown targets, but the group-level difference was not statistically conclusive. The methodological choices highlight the need for more experimentally validated synthesis benchmarks and position LLMs as a thermally informed prior.

    DSADiff: Data Structure-Aware Diffusion for Crystal Structure Prediction via Mitigating Complexity- and Imbalance-Induced Biases

    Authors
    Jiang Xiaobo, Dinghong Lai
    Published
    2026-11-17
    Category
    cs.LG
    arXiv ID
    2611.22050v1
    Open on arXiv ↗
    Read abstract

    Crystal Structure Prediction (CSP) is an important step in accelerating the generation and discovery of new materials. In recent years, diffusion-based CSP methods have made significant progress. However, existing models still suffer from generation bias when facing diverse materials spaces, while systematic approaches for tracing the sources of such performance variations remain limited. To address this issue, we propose LAS (Layered Analysis Strategy), a mechanism analysis framework that identifies crystal complexity and elemental composition imbalance as two key factors affecting generation difficulty and further reveals their relationships with model generation performance. Building on these findings, we further propose DSADiff (Data Structure-Aware Diffusion), a data structure-aware diffusion model that transforms the key factors identified by LAS into a unified generation difficulty representation and uses it to jointly drive feature modeling and training strategy optimization. This enables the model to adapt its representation and generation capability to samples with different data structures and difficulty levels, thereby mitigating learning bias across samples. Experiments on three CSP benchmark datasets, including Carbon-24, demonstrate that DSADiff consistently improves both Match Rate and RMSE, with a maximum Match Rate improvement of 9%, surpassing existing state-of-the-art methods and achieving new state-of-the-art performance. This work deepens the understanding of diffusion-based CSP models from the perspective of generation bias and provides a new direction for designing less biased generative models for complex materials spaces.

    Generative models play a rapidly growing role in the design and simulation of molecules. The most widely used applications of generative models are in large language models (LLMs), owing to the natural flexibility of language for specifying and answering queries. However, generalist LLMs do not effectively handle large sets of 3D coordinates, whereas specialist generative models of 3D molecules cannot reach the controllability afforded by language. To bridge these capabilities, we propose Nahual, a decoder-only autoregressive diffusion model for any sequence of text and 3D molecules. Our results demonstrate the viability of scaling generalist decoder-only models for native multimodal understanding and generation across language and chemistry.     CGTransformer: A Topology-Aware Autoregressive Framework for Crystal Generation and Inverse Design

    Authors
    Zhennan Zhang, Shaobo Yu, Haoting Zhang, Yu Han, Zhiyue Guo, Junjie Wang, Hao Gao, Jian Sun
    Published
    2026-11-13
    Category
    physics.comp-ph
    arXiv ID
    2611.18303v1
    Open on arXiv ↗
    Read abstract

    Autoregressive language models offer a unified route to crystal generation and inverse design, but their performance depends on how periodic structures are serialized. Symmetry equivalent and closely related crystals can receive different atomic orders under coordinate-based serialization, introducing variability unrelated to their structural connectivity. Here, we present CGTransformer (Crystal Graph Transformer), an autoregressive generative framework that addresses this challenge via a graph-embedded sorting strategy. Using quotientgraph topology and barycentric embeddings, Element Symmetry Graph (ESG) converts periodic crystal networks into topology-guided one-dimensional sequences that retain key local and symmetry-related structural information. CGTransformer achieves high generation validity and the highest single shot crystal structure prediction (CSP) match rate among the compared MP-20 baselines (63.24%), while same backbone comparisons show improved structure recovery over coordinate-based ordering. Furthermore, we demonstrate property-conditioned inverse design for wide-bandgap semiconductors, where ESG achieves a much higher stable, unique and novel (S.U.N.) rate and stronger target-property alignment than the coordinate-based baseline. These results establish topology-guided sequence construction as an effective route to improving crystal generation and property-directed design at fixed model capacity.

    Computational modelling has become a cornerstone of materials science, with advances in atomistic simulations, high-throughput computation, and artificial intelligence transforming how materials are understood, discovered, and designed. These developments build upon generations of theoretical innovations that have translated complex mathematical and physical principles into practical predictive tools. However, the growing diversity of computational methods presents challenges for practitioners seeking a coherent understanding of their theoretical foundations. Drawing upon the author's research experience, this monograph provides a practitioner-oriented, pedagogical overview of computational materials science, unified through the concepts of potential-energy landscapes and statistical sampling. By connecting established physical theories with emerging data-driven approaches, this monograph offers a unified perspective on computational materials science and identifies opportunities and challenges toward physics-informed, efficient, and reliable materials discovery.

    Crystal Structure Prediction from Search to Learning: A 30-Year Expedition

    Authors
    Jianjun Hu, Ying Feng
    Published
    2026-11-13
    Category
    cond-mat.mtrl-sci
    arXiv ID
    2611.17670v1
    Open on arXiv ↗
    Read abstract

    Crystal structure prediction (CSP) determines periodic atomic or molecular arrangements from chemical composition and physical conditions. Over three decades, CSP has evolved from chemical construction and global energy optimization to hybrid approaches combining symmetry, learned potentials, adaptive search, and deep generative models. This survey reviews method, software, review, and benchmark records from 1996 to October 2026, covering inorganic and molecular crystals, including formula-conditioned diffusion, flow, and autoregressive models that preserve composition. We classify methods by candidate construction and exploration: template and discrete construction, random search, evolutionary search, swarm and differential evolution, energy-landscape exploration, adaptive machine-learning-guided search, and learned generation. We distinguish representation, relaxation, ranking, and physical qualification. Benchmarks reveal no universally dominant approach. In a 46-target leaderboard snapshot, the best-performing template method achieves higher top-1 recovery than leading generative models, but paired tests show no statistically significant differences from several generative competitors. We also audit predictions for 68 experimentally examined materials, including 52 with structures confirmed by diffraction or microscopy. Predictions for 23 of the 68 preceded experiment, mostly through first-principles global search. Within the reviewed evidence, we find no independently confirmed discovery in which a deep generative model predicted the structure before experiment. We conclude with the principal limitations of current CSP algorithms and priorities for future research.

    Diffusion Tree Sampling for Inference Time Adaptation of Crystal Generative Models

    Authors
    Daniel T. Levy, Vineet Jain, Tara Akhound-Sadegh, Sékou-Oumar Kaba, Siamak Ravanbakhsh
    Published
    2026-11-12
    Category
    cs.LG
    arXiv ID
    2611.17462v1
    Open on arXiv ↗
    Read abstract

    Diffusion-based generative models have become strong priors over plausible crystal structures, but turning that prior into promising candidates requires steering toward several conflicting properties at once. Fine-tuning is expensive and produces a new model for every new objective, while differentiable guidance is not applicable to many real-world tasks. We instead adapt these generative models at inference time using Diffusion Tree Sampling (DTS), a Monte Carlo tree search procedure over the denoising trajectory. We make two technical contributions on top of DTS that are particularly relevant for AI-for-science applications. First, we show how to use existing property-conditional generative models (e.g., models fine-tuned for classifier-free guidance) as guided proposals inside DTS, without distorting the target distribution. Second, we extend DTS to multi-objective sampling, so that a single search produces samples spanning an entire Pareto front. We instantiate both adaptations on de novo crystal generation: improving the stability of crystals generated by different models, using pretrained conditional models to generate crystals belonging to rare space groups or with high bulk modulus, and generating crystals along a Pareto front of high dielectric constant and high bandgap materials. Across these tasks, DTS-based inference-time adaptation successfully improves model outputs without modifying the models themselves.

    ORCHID-SAGE: Fully Reproducible CALPHAD Assessment from Evidence to Phase Diagram

    Authors
    Raymundo Arróyave
    Published
    2026-11-12
    Category
    cond-mat.mtrl-sci
    arXiv ID
    2611.16308v1
    Open on arXiv ↗
    Read abstract

    Knowledge of phase stability is the first requirement for materials design, and predicting the conditions under which new materials can be synthesized has become a key capability of self-driving laboratories (SDLs). For inorganic materials, notably alloys, these predictions come from thermodynamic databases built with the CALPHAD method. Building a database, however, requires expert judgment at many steps, and because these decisions are rarely recorded, others can use a database but cannot regenerate or update it. Machines can already collect the data, build a first model with machine-learned interatomic potentials and refine it against experiments. The assessment itself, the sequence of judgments that turns these parts into a reliable database, is still carried out by human experts. We argue that it can be automated reproducibly only if the expert knowledge behind an assessment is encoded in formal protocols, so that an assessment delivers a record of its construction and not only its parameters. We introduce ORCHID-SAGE, a standard that organizes this record into reproducible and automatable protocols. In this first attempt, applied to Cr-Ni, the database was regenerated from its record by a script on a second machine, by an AI agent working only through the Model Context Protocol (MCP) and by a refit from public sources only, with the same predictions. Recorded in this way, a database need no longer be fixed at publication but can be updated by AI agents as SDLs and other sources provide new evidence, while the judgment remains with people and on the record.

    Quantum Material Generation via Coherent Hamiltonian Search

    Authors
    Jiang Yuhan, Matthew Otten
    Published
    2026-11-11
    Category
    quant-ph
    arXiv ID
    2611.16016v1
    Open on arXiv ↗
    Read abstract

    Discovering new materials with targeted properties is a central goal of materials science, yet the space of candidates is vast. Data-driven generative models learn the distribution of known materials, but they extrapolate poorly, and a generated structure is not known to be stable. A material's properties follow from its Hamiltonian, and a quantum computer can calculate them from it. We introduce the quantum material generative algorithm (QMage), a coherent search, guided by the Hamiltonian, over crystalline configurations of atomic composition, unit cell structure, and atomic positions, for one whose calculated properties meet the targets to within a given error tolerance. The computation is a single coherent quantum circuit in which each configuration's Hamiltonian is block-encoded, its target eigenstate isolated and properties evaluated, and the satisfying configurations amplified by fixed-point search and measured. One framework covers many targets, from formation energy and band gaps to elasticity and conductivity. We design the algorithm for the mature fault-tolerant era and estimate its cost in logical qubits and Toffoli depth from a classically routine benchmark to targets where established methods are unreliable. The approach generates candidate materials from first principles, rather than generalizing from those known, and could enable materials discovery when the target properties are difficult to predict classically.

    Authors
    Jiho Lee, Seungwoo Hwang, Seungwu Han, Jung-Hoon Lee, Jisu Jung, Youngho Kang, Sungwoo Kang
    Published
    2026-11-10
    Category
    cond-mat.mtrl-sci
    arXiv ID
    2611.13611v2
    Open on arXiv ↗
    Read abstract

    Mixed-anion lithium oxychlorides exhibit high Li conductivity, but the relationship between oxygen content, structural framework, and Li-ion transport remains unclear. Here, crystal structure prediction and molecular dynamics simulations with machine-learning interatomic potentials are used to examine $\mathrm{Li}_yM\mathrm{O}_x\mathrm{Cl}_{6-2x}$ ($M$ = Nb and Ta [Group 5] and $M$ = Ti, Zr, and Hf [Group 4]). Increasing oxygen content changes isolated chloride-rich polyhedra into one-dimensional chains and subsequently more highly connected two- and three-dimensional frameworks. In the Group 5 series, fast Li diffusion occurs over $x \approx 0.67$--1.5, where the Li sites form a three-dimensional percolating network. Potential of mean force maps and nudged elastic band calculations reveal that fast Li-ion transport requires a low-dimensional framework supporting long-range migration through undercoordinated Li sites with low barriers, together with similar energies across Li sites. Finally, we perform fine-grid compositional search, which identifies $\mathrm{LiNbO}_{0.67}\mathrm{Cl}_{4.67}$, $\mathrm{LiTaO}_{0.75}\mathrm{Cl}_{4.5}$, $\mathrm{LiNbO}_{1.5}\mathrm{Cl}_3$, and $\mathrm{LiTaO}_{1.5}\mathrm{Cl}_3$ as stable candidates with predicted room-temperature self-diffusivities of up to $\sim 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{cm}^2/\mathrm{s}$. These findings establish how oxygen content controls framework and Li-ion transport, guiding the design of lithium oxyhalide electrolytes.

    Published
    2026-11-07

    arXiv ID

    Read abstract

    Crystal Structure Prediction (CSP) aims to predict a material's 3D structure from its chemical composition, with applications from energy storage to biomedical devices. Despite its importance, existing evaluation frameworks suffer from key limitations: they rely on threshold-sensitive or incomparable metrics, focus predominantly on geometric performance while overlooking physical plausibility, and fail to account for polymorphism, where a chemical composition can crystallize into multiple stable structures. In parallel, recent Symmetry-Informed CSP (SICSP) methods often report results alongside standard CSP baselines while receiving crystallographic templates at inference, which constrain the search space and make SICSP a fundamentally different task. To address these limitations, we introduce an Orientation-invariant, Polymorph-Aware, Large Benchmark (OPAL-Bench) that evaluates CSP and SICSP as separate tasks. We evaluate 7 CSP and 2 SICSP methods under OPAL-Bench across 4 datasets using threshold-free geometric and physical distance metrics. Our results show that no method dominates across all metrics, and that geometric performance is not strongly correlated with physical plausibility. We also release OPAL-CSP, an SE(3)-equivariant flow-matching baseline for the CSP track that performs competitively on geometric metrics and ranks among the best on physical ones. By unifying geometric and physical metrics under a polymorph-aware protocol, OPAL-Bench enables a more comprehensive assessment of generative quality in CSP.

    Data-driven candidate structure ensemble generation beyond the convex hull with crystALL approach

    Authors
    Adam M. Krajewski, Ricardo Amaral, Jonathan W. Siegel, Luke A. Myers, Rushi Gong, Shun-Li Shang, Zi-Kui Liu
    Published
    2026-11-02
    Category
    cond-mat.mtrl-sci
    arXiv ID
    2611.02584v1
    Open on arXiv ↗
    Read abstract

    Real materials consist of multiple stable or metastable states under real-world conditions, existing as statistical ergodic ensembles of configurations rather than ideal crystals. Yet, data-driven computational materials discovery increasingly biases itself toward finding "new stable structures" from ground-state DFT calculations alone. This limits our long-term ability to discover materials whose properties emerge from metastable phases, to explain experimental observations, or to model thermodynamic behavior comprehensively. Predicting ergodic sets of atomic configurations of known and hypothetical solids is a fundamental challenge in theoretical crystallography, approached via many complementary methods. We discuss this conceptually and introduce crystALL, a pragmatic and powerful data-driven approach to discovering ensembles beyond the convex hull, embracing structural diversity over stability alone, enabling robust experiment matching in cases evading traditional methods. crystALL leverages 5 million relaxed structures from OPTIMADE and MPDD to rapidly generate a comprehensive candidate set by: (1) species substitutions; (2) ML feature-space analysis with pySIPFENN; (3) representative structure selection via clustering and energy-minimization or property matching; and (4) construction of the final candidate ensemble for experimental or ab initio validation. We also uniquely address the common challenge of experimental composition uncertainties spanning many integer stoichiometries by new efficient searches across anonymized compositional ranges rather than fixed formulas. Overall, crystALL's ensemble-focused philosophy provides critical advantages over the prevailing convex-hull-searching paradigm, which often fails to account for key experimental observations and increases AI/ML discovery bias over time - akin to model collapse in LLMs retrained on iteratively generated content.

While existing generative models for crystals can generate materials with charge-neutral oxidation-state assignments, they poorly reproduce the distributions of oxidation states observed in synthesised materials. To address this limitation, we propose OxiGen, an oxidation-state-aware crystal diffusion model that explicitly represents oxidation states during generation. OxiGen enforces global charge neutrality by construction using a structured output layer with exact inference over a finite-state automaton. Empirically, OxiGen substantially improves oxidation-state fidelity, generates the highest rate of stable, unique, and novel crystals among evaluated methods, and maintains high compositional validity even under property conditioning.
