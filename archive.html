    TCSP3.0: Template-Based Crystal Structure Prediction with Coefficient-Preserving Element Substitution and Structural Diversity

    Authors
    Ying Feng, Jeffrey Hu, Lai Wei, Liang Tao, Madeline Miklos, Dipannoy Das Gupta, Hossein Erfanshekooh, Jianjun Hu
    Published
    2026-10-25
    Category
    cond-mat.mtrl-sci
    arXiv ID
    2610.34029v1
    Open on arXiv ↗
    Read abstract

    Template-based crystal structure prediction (CSP) transfers a known crystal structure to a new composition by element substitution, so its accuracy depends on retrieving suitable templates, mapping elements and ranking the resulting candidates correctly. Auditing the original TCSP2.0 implementation on the T180 benchmark, we found that 469 of its 2,347 selected structures (20%) have the wrong composition, caused by element mappings that ignore stoichiometric coefficients and by sequential text substitution that cascades or corrupts element symbols. We propose TCSP3.0, which enforces coefficient-preserving mappings, substitutes species simultaneously on parsed structures and validates every candidate's composition and cell. It further adds periodic-number retrieval, soft oxidation-state evidence, radius-compatibility scoring, structural deduplication, and ORB-v3 machine-learning-potential relaxation and energy ranking of its candidates, as used by deep generative CSP baselines. TCSP3.0 achieves Top-1 and Top-20 match rates of 72.2% and 90.6% on T180, compared with 28.3% and 45.6% for TCSP2.0, and a Top-20 match rate of 74.0% on a new 800-target benchmark (T800). On T180 it also outperforms PNcsp+ and deep generative models CrystalFlow and MaskGXT (Top-1 72.2% versus at most 63.9%). Even without relaxation, TCSP3.0 reaches 67.8% at Top-1 and 90.6% at Top-20 in a median of 28 s per target on two CPU cores, about ten times faster than CrystalFlow. Paired ten-arm ablations on T180 and T800 show that most of the improvement comes from the correctness fixes; structural deduplication significantly improves the Top-5 match rate, embedding-based retrieval is required for coverage, and the remaining scoring features have small or null effects.

    Exploring Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures for Self-Supervised Representation Learning of Powder X-ray Diffraction Patterns

    Authors
    Ming-Chiang Chang, Brian DeCost
    Published
    2026-10-23
    Category
    cond-mat.mtrl-sci
    arXiv ID
    2610.33310v1
    Open on arXiv ↗
    Read abstract

    Powder X-ray diffraction (XRD) is the primary high-throughput method for resolving crystal structures, a cornerstone of materials science. As high-throughput synthesis and self-driving laboratories advance, automated XRD pattern analysis has become vital for accelerating materials discovery. However, traditional matrix factorization methods fail when lattice variations cause non-linear peak shifts. At the same time, supervised learning approaches face a combinatorial explosion when handling multi-phase mixtures. To overcome these challenges, we adopt a self-supervised Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) to learn representation embeddings from 1D diffraction patterns. By optimizing a masked self-prediction loss directly within the latent space, the framework captures high-level crystallographic semantics. Downstream evaluations show that lightweight attentive probes trained on frozen JEPA representations achieve space group classification and lattice parameter estimation accuracy close to much larger supervised vision transformers. Furthermore, latent probing reveals that the learned representation space naturally organizes physical transformations, isolating smooth trajectories for peak broadening. Interestingly, decoding along latent paths between single-phase crystal representations yields realistic multi-phase mixture patterns, demonstrating an emergent continuous structure learned without explicit multi-phase supervision.

    Orbitol: Crystal Generation on Wyckoff Orbits

    Authors
    Ivan Žugec, Joshua Rosenthal, Ivor Lončarić, Jan-Willem van de Meent, Tin Hadži Veljković
    Published
    2026-10-21
    Category
    cs.LG
    arXiv ID
    2610.30086v1
    Open on arXiv ↗
    Read abstract

    Generative models propose crystalline materials by learning composition and geometry, but unconditional sampling gives no direct control over the space group, which constrains whether responses such as piezoelectricity are symmetry-allowed. We present Orbitol, a crystal generator built on the Crystalite diffusion Transformer that replaces atom tokens with Wyckoff-orbit tokens. Given a space group and a collection of occupied Wyckoff positions, Orbitol generates the atom type and free coordinates of each orbit together with a compatible lattice, so every valid structure is invariant under the operations of the requested group. It generates structures two to three orders of magnitude faster than the symmetry-aware baselines at the evaluated settings. On MP-20, 13.8% of Orbitol's attempts yielded stable or metastable, unique and novel structures that retained the requested space group after relaxation, compared with 10.8% for SGFM, the next best model. We also introduce SPARC, a score that combines retention of the raw detected space group with agreement between the retained-group and training distributions. Finally, we used Orbitol to generate candidates in piezoelectric crystal classes. Of the structures selected by internal screening, 89% passed our density functional theory checks, and 79% also matched no Materials Project or Alexandria entry with the same space group. Their median figure of merit was nine times that of the piezoelectric materials computed in the Materials Project, and 24% of the new candidates ranked in that set's top 10%.

    CrystalAR: Autoregressive Modeling for Symmetric Crystal Generation

    Authors
    Ziqiao Meng, Rongsheng Chen, Zhi Zheng, Nikita Kazeev, Zhenkun Wang, Andrey Ustyuzhanin, Wee Sun Lee
    Published
    2026-10-20
    Category
    cs.AI
    arXiv ID
    2610.27762v2
    Open on arXiv ↗
    Read abstract

    Deep generative modeling has achieved remarkable progress in crystal generation. Given the rich symmetries inherent in crystalline structures, recent work has sought to explicitly incorporate space-group symmetry into generative models, enabling more effective exploration of novel symmetry templates. Yet introducing symmetry templates increases the combinatorial complexity of stoichiometric relationships between atomic species and crystallographic sites, while creating a fundamental tension between flexible exploration of discrete crystallographic templates and faithful realization of these templates as continuous three-dimensional structures. To address these challenges, we propose CrystalAR, a unified autoregressive framework that models Wyckoff symmetry templates and complete crystal structures within a single generative sequence. Specifically, CrystalAR reformulates continuous coordinate generation as a symmetry-conditioned hierarchy of discrete spatial localization decisions, autoregressively refining the positions of all symmetry-inequivalent sites across resolution levels while jointly modeling their spatial configuration within each level. This discrete hierarchical formulation further enables constraint-aware search over both crystallographic templates and geometric realizations. As a result, CrystalAR retains strong symmetry-template generation capability while achieving competitive de novo generation performance and template-free crystal structure prediction (CSP) accuracy. These results suggest that symmetry-template generation can be retained alongside broadly competitive de novo generation performance and competitive CSP without reliance on symmetry templates retrieved from prior data, providing a promising alternative paradigm for symmetry-preserving crystal generation.

    A Comprehensive Survey of Crystal Structural Prototypes: Construction, Classification, and Applications

    Authors
    Jianjun Hu, Ying Feng, Rongzhi Dong, Jeffrey Hu, Remy Rogers, Dipannoy Gupta
    Published
    2026-10-15
    Category
    cond-mat.mtrl-sci
    arXiv ID
    2610.22600v1
    Open on arXiv ↗
    Read abstract

    Crystal structural prototypes describe recurring atomic arrangements across chemically distinct materials. Prototype libraries are built through crystallographic curation, standardization, geometric matching, local-environment clustering, and periodic-graph analysis, and support chemical substitution, derivative enumeration, high-throughput screening, structure solution, crystal structure prediction (CSP), and generative design. This survey compares methods by the structural information they retain and the equivalence rules they impose, distinguishing crystallographic structure types, geometric realizations, chemistry-anonymous topologies, and chemical decorations. We review curated and automatically extracted prototype libraries (AFLOW, CSPD, LAE-ICSPD, EMFDTW, TYPIX, ICSD structure types), their source databases, and algorithms for assigning family membership. A GraphID-based analysis of samples from ICSD, the Materials Project, OQMD, and LeMat shows that the 100 most frequent families in each sample cover 30 to 67 percent of analyzed structures, while 72 to 82 percent of observed families are singletons. The GraphIDs of up to 76 percent of structures change when the neighbor tolerance is varied. In our comparison of 12 generative CSP models, template retrieval achieves the highest top-1 success rate. Removing a reduced-stoichiometry class from training introduces new failures in 50 to 78 percent of the test structures of that class, which measures sensitivity to stoichiometric coverage, not generalization to unseen prototypes. We relate these findings to earlier statistics of structure types, space groups, and periodic nets, and to protein-fold coverage studies. We propose independent-database coverage measurements, discovery-rate analyses, and family-held-out tests to assess how prototype reuse affects prediction accuracy, generalization, and novelty.

    Risk-Controlled DFT Selection for Machine-Learned Interatomic Potentials

    Authors
    Kun Ryu, Yeo Jin Jung, Shyue Ping Ong, Claire Donnat, Ying Shirley Meng
    Published
    2026-10-14
    Category
    stat.ML
    arXiv ID
    2610.19630v1
    Open on arXiv ↗
    Read abstract

    Automated materials discovery requires evaluating large pools of candidate configurations, with density functional theory (DFT) as a major throughput bottleneck. Pretrained machine-learned interatomic potentials (MLIPs) approximate DFT cheaply, but their errors vary by orders of magnitude across configurations, making it difficult to decide when to trust the MLIP predictions and when to fall back to DFT. We frame this decision as a risk-controlled selection problem and solve it within a conformal risk control (CRC) framework. Each configuration is mapped from atomic numbers and Cartesian coordinates to a fixed geometric descriptor, which a lightweight predictor uses to estimate an MLIP-DFT disagreement score in energy, average force, and maximum force. CRC then calibrates this score into a selection threshold for DFT labeling such that the MLIP error on the unselected configurations, which keep their MLIP labels, stays below a user-chosen fraction $α$ of the expected error. Under an identical guarantee across eight dataset-MLIP pairs, our predictor routes 7% and 14% fewer configurations to DFT at $α=0.3$ than committee baselines. Because committee disagreement overlooks errors the MLIPs share, our gains are largest when the DFT labels and MLIP training data differ in DFT functional and basis set, and narrow when the two agree. Without model access, the structure-only predictor also ranks errors as well as the target MLIP's latent representation and outperforms widely used descriptors such as Smooth Overlap of Atomic Positions (SOAP) and Atom-Centered Symmetry Functions (ACSF).

    CondMSP: Condition-Informed and Resource-Aware Inorganic Materials Synthesis Planning

    Authors
    Heewoong Noh, Gyoung S. Na, Elton Pan, Namkyeong Lee, Chanyoung Park
    Published
    2026-10-11
    Category
    cs.AI
    arXiv ID
    2610.15603v1
    Open on arXiv ↗
    Read abstract

    Materials synthesis planning (MSP) connects AI-driven materials discovery to experimental realization by proposing precursors, synthesis operations, and processing conditions for a target material. Existing approaches predict these components separately or in pairs and treat conditions only as a prediction target. Consequently, MSP remains centered on recipe prediction: it leaves unexplored how conditions can serve as process context for constructing synthesis routes, and it does not address which candidate routes should be executed under limited experimental resources. We propose CondMSP, which extends MSP from recipe prediction to execution-aware planning. CondMSP predicts temperature, time, and atmosphere by combining a joint predictor with variable-specific experts, and then uses these predictions both as process context, grounding the generation and retrieval of operation sequences, and as execution requirements, specifying which thermal stages can share a furnace run and at what cost. This specification is held fixed across all compared methods; within it, CondMSP ranks routes across precursor sets by a common utility and, under furnace budgets, jointly selects routes and allocates their thermal stages by constrained optimization. CondMSP improves condition and operation prediction and, under this shared constraint, raises reference-recipe recovery from 27.9% to 32.9% at three simulated furnace runs per target and from 34.7% to 40.0% at 30 trials, while a new evaluation set of 2,344 targets supports generalization beyond oxide-dominated training chemistry.

    Inverse Designing Reconfigurable Piezoelectric Tensors via Architectural and Modal Programming

    Authors
    Anish Satpati, Marco Maurizi, Hyunmoon Cho, Victor Couedel, Nicolas Mule, Xiaoyu, Zheng
    Published
    2026-10-10
    Category
    cond-mat.mtrl-sci
    arXiv ID
    2610.14761v1
    Open on arXiv ↗
    Read abstract

    Piezoelectricity arises from the coupling between electric polarization and mechanical deformation, described by a third-rank tensor whose coefficients are constrained by crystal symmetry. In natural crystals, atomic periodicity and energetic stability restrict accessible symmetries, limited to 20 distinct anisotropies that can exist. Here we establish a general framework, based on graph representation, for controlling the full effective piezoelectric tensor through architecture by resolving how macroscopic symmetry, local polarization and deformation of structural elements act together. This enables each coefficient to be traced to the members that reinforce or cancel it and be controlled through targeted geometric changes. We further uncover frequency-selective tensor reconfiguration, in which excitation frequency switches the dominant piezoelectric coefficients of a single architecture. These principles open a broader class of applications in which a single piezoelectric architecture can deliver programmable sensing, actuation and mode conversion across multiple directions and deformation states. We realize a multi-degree-of-freedom actuator that integrates distinct actuation modes in one block and reduces volume by 90% relative to conventional multi-stack assemblies.

    Large Out-of-plane Polarization in Two-dimensional Bi2O3 Monolayers Predicted by an AI-driven Inverse-design Workflow

    Authors
    Hongmei Tan, Jing Zhang, Shan Gao, Yueyu Zhang, Xiangmei Duan
    Published
    2026-10-10
    Category
    cond-mat.mtrl-sci
    arXiv ID
    2610.14429v1
    Open on arXiv ↗
    Read abstract

    Two-dimensional (2D) polar oxides with built-in electric fields offer promising opportunities for nanoscale electronics, optoelectronics, and surface charge engineering. However, their discovery remains challenging due to the enormous structural complexity of nonlayered oxide networks. Here, we combine inverse structural design with a machine-learning interatomic potential and first-principles calculations to explore 2D oxide configurations. Using Bi2O3 as a representative system, we identify two previously unreported polar monolayers with tetragonal and orthorhombic symmetries. Both structures exhibit vertically separated Bi and O sublayers, giving rise to pronounced intrinsic polarity with Berry-phase 2D polarizations of 34.12 and 37.15 pC/m, respectively. Notably, the o-Bi2O3 monolayer exhibits one of the largest reported out-of-plane polarizations among 2D oxides. Both monolayers exhibit dynamical stability, finite-temperature structural integrity, and wide band gaps (~3.8 eV). The large polarization originates from dimensionality-induced reconstruction of the nonlayered Bi-O network, which breaks local coordination symmetry and produces out-of-plane charge redistribution, while the stereochemically active Bi 6s2 lone pair further favors asymmetric Bi-O bonding. These findings suggest dimensionality-induced local-coordination symmetry breaking as a promising design motif for high-polarization 2D oxides, and demonstrate the utility of combining inverse design with machine-learning-assisted screening to explore unconventional polar phases in nonlayered oxide networks.

    ManifoldCache: Training-Free Diffusion Acceleration via Constraint Manifold Caching

    Authors
    Prashant Pandey, Devineni Sri Venkatraya Chowdary, Brejesh Lall
    Published
    2026-10-03
    Category
    cs.AI
    arXiv ID
    2610.04510v1
    Open on arXiv ↗
    Read abstract

    Diffusion models for structured scientific generation must produce samples satisfying hard geometric constraints imposed by physics, chemistry, or biology, yet inference in these settings is prohibitively slow, demanding hundreds to thousands of neural-function evaluations per sample. We unify eight state-of-the-art models spanning medical volumetrics, molecular conformations, protein backbone design, crystal structure prediction, and multi-view 3D scenes under a single abstraction, Constraint-Manifold Diffusion Models (CMDMs), in which the target distribution is supported on a manifold defined by an externally specified constraint map. All existing acceleration families fail on this class: quantization exhausts memory on high-dimensional volumetric operators; pruning breaks constraint fidelity; fast ODE solvers allow trajectories to drift off the constraint manifold; and feature-caching heuristics are blind to constraint geometry, inducing mode confusion in the high-noise regime. We introduce ManifoldCache, the first training-free, data-free accelerator designed from first principles for CMDMs. The key insight is that the conditional score decomposes orthogonally into a normal component, which enforces constraint satisfaction, and a tangential component, which navigates within the manifold. Exploiting this structure, we prove that the noise-schedule midpoint is a sharp safe-caching boundary: caching before it incurs provably bounded error, while caching after it guarantees a strictly positive fraction of trajectories suffer mode confusion, a gap that persists up to the boundary. We further prove that deeper network blocks admit provably larger certified cache strides within the safe phase, as a consequence of the score decomposition propagating through block Jacobians. The resulting schedule requires no calibration data, along with zero training overhead.

    Authors
    Qiang Zhu, Yihan Weng
    Published
    2026-10-03
    Category
    cond-mat.mtrl-sci
    arXiv ID
    2610.04193v1
    Open on arXiv ↗
    Read abstract

    A molecular crystal structure is jointly described by its space group symmetry, unit cell, and the molecular alignment within the asymmetric unit. Concurrently predicting all three variables is a daunting task, as it mixes discrete symmetry choices with a high-dimensional search in the continuous space. To address this challenge, we decouple these variables using a three-step generation process. Specifically, we train a flow model to learn the conditional distribution of invariant lattice descriptors (e.g. direct- and reciprocal-lattice successive minima and Selling scalars) from a molecular graph, a Hall setting, and the number of molecules in the asymmetric unit ($Z'$).     Inverse materials design is a long-standing goal of computational materials discovery. Generative models for crystalline materials are typically trained to match the distribution of a structure database, while nothing in their training objective points them at specific design goals such as targeted properties. We use group-relative policy optimization (GRPO) to align a generative model based on stochastic interpolants and discrete flow matching with general black-box reward functions through reinforcement learning.     Generative models have made rapid progress in ordered crystal structure prediction, yet many functional materials are intrinsically disordered, with substitutional mixing, vacancies, or interstitial species controlling their properties. Existing crystal generators either assume deterministic site occupations or require site-level disorder annotations, which are often unavailable when the chemical formula is the primary input. We formulate disordered crystal structure prediction through an Occupancy Distribution Matrix (ODM), a continuous site-by-species representation that unifies ordered crystals, solid solutions, vacancy disorder, and interstitial occupancy.
